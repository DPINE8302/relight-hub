## Story and camera rules

1. **Every projected shot is subjective POV.** The audience never sees the protagonist from outside, in a mirror, or as a third-person silhouette. Hands, breathing, camera height, and movement carry age and emotion.
2. Scene 02 is a sequence of *possible lives across years*, not a literal trip through Bangkok in one afternoon. Clothing may change in the possibility montage. P11 returns to the present-day high-school uniform and the established school hallway/classroom.
3. The first wake at W00 opens the dream of possibilities: morning light crosses the child's familiar room, then the beam and window match into a teenage bedroom view before P01. The final wake at R03 ends that possible-future dream. Familiar bedroom geometry links both, while the physical room becomes the visitor's reality after projected black.
4. August, Guy, and Nam are the three main recurring friends who bring the vaping offer. Their warmth is real and their social pressure is legible; play them as grounded teenagers rather than cartoon villains. Guy says both locked offer lines. The first use has no instant horror beat. Dependency develops across repeated days.
5. Basketball, editing, and music callbacks show this **character's feared possible future**. The film does not assert permanent disability or a guaranteed outcome from one use.
6. The collapse is a dramatic event under medical evaluation. The doctor does not diagnose lung injury, name a disease, or claim the vape definitively caused the collapse. The educational claims are confined to reviewed facts about nicotine and aerosol.
7. Build clean visual plates with their shot-specific room tone, motivated effects, music, and exact dialogue cues inside the generation brief. Verify every intelligible Thai line and replace inaccurate generated speech with clean actor recording in controlled post-production. Exact Thai on-screen text, legible interfaces, school marks, health words, visitor recording, grading, and final transitions are finished in post-production.
